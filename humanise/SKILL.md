# Humanise

Rewrite text for a real human reader without turning it into generic “professional” copy.

## Identify the situation

Infer or establish:

- who is speaking;
- who will read it;
- the channel: email, chat, document, post, meeting note, or spoken answer;
- the relationship and power distance;
- the purpose: inform, ask, persuade, disagree, update, apologize, or decide;
- the desired level of formality.

When the user already gives enough context, do not ask questions just to fill a template.

## Preserve the author's intent

Keep facts, numbers, names, dates, commitments, important qualifiers, point of view, the requested ask or decision, meaningful emotion, and domain language the audience understands.

Never invent evidence, accomplishments, urgency, or confidence.

## Make it sound spoken by a person

Prefer direct subjects and verbs, familiar words, contractions when appropriate, varied sentence lengths, specific details, and transitions a person would actually say.

Avoid:

- “I hope this message finds you well” unless requested;
- corporate filler;
- fake enthusiasm;
- heavy nominalization;
- symmetrical “firstly/secondly/thirdly” structure when normal prose works;
- overuse of dashes;
- repeating the ask in the closing line.

## Keep the right amount of imperfection

A good human message is not always maximally polished. For chats and internal messages, small fragments and simple wording can make the writing more believable and easier to read.

Do not add spelling mistakes, fake typos, or forced slang.

## Channel guidance

### Chat / DM
Get to the point quickly. Keep context only if the recipient needs it.

### Email
Use a useful subject, then context → ask/update → next step. Keep formalities proportional to the relationship.

### Product or work document
Write for scanning but keep the narrative intact. Use headings only where they help navigation.

### Interview or spoken answer
Use natural spoken structure: situation → what I did → why → result → learning.

### Public post
Keep a clear point of view and concrete examples. Do not flatten personality into generic thought-leadership language.

## Final check

1. Can the reader identify the point quickly?
2. Does it sound like the same person, just clearer?
3. Are there words the author would probably never say?
4. Did the rewrite exaggerate or soften the original meaning?
5. Is the ask or conclusion obvious?
6. Is the tone right for this relationship and channel?

Return the finished text. Explain edits only when the user asks.

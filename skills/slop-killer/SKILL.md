# Slop Killer

Edit writing so it sounds like a capable person wrote it for a real reader.

## Preserve before cutting

Keep the author's point of view, concrete facts, examples, numbers, constraints, useful uncertainty, domain terms, deliberate humor, and required caveats.

Do not make writing shorter by deleting the substance that makes it credible.

## Remove common AI patterns

Cut or rewrite:

- empty openings such as “In today's fast-paced world”;
- praise before the answer;
- repeated summaries of the same point;
- “not just X, but Y” when a direct sentence works;
- fake contrasts and forced three-part lists;
- generic claims such as “this is crucial” without explaining why;
- unnecessary “key,” “robust,” “seamless,” “holistic,” “comprehensive,” “leverage,” and “empower” language;
- headings for every two sentences;
- excessive em dashes, colons, and parenthetical asides;
- abstract nouns where a verb is clearer;
- meta commentary about the response itself;
- conclusions that merely repeat the introduction.

## Prefer concrete writing

Replace abstract claims with actors, actions, evidence, and outcomes.

Example:

```text
Avoid: The solution enables enhanced operational efficiency.
Prefer: The team can now review failed requests in one place instead of checking three dashboards.
```

## Match the surface

### Chat or message
Use short sentences, normal contractions, and the vocabulary the team already uses.

### Product or technical document
Keep enough structure for scanning. Preserve definitions, decisions, trade-offs, edge cases, metrics, and owners.

### Public writing
Keep personality and rhythm. Avoid polishing every sentence into the same length and cadence.

### Executive update
Lead with outcome, evidence, risk, and next decision. Remove implementation detail unless it changes the decision.

## Editing pass

1. Identify the single main point.
2. Delete sentences that do not support it.
3. Combine repeated ideas.
4. Replace abstractions with concrete actors, actions, and outcomes.
5. Remove unsupported certainty and exaggerated claims.
6. Simplify headings and list structure.
7. Vary sentence length and keep natural transitions.
8. Check that no important fact, caveat, or decision disappeared.

## Do not over-correct

Natural writing can be imperfect. Keep fragments, informal words, or repetition when they carry voice or emphasis. Do not introduce slang the author would not use. Do not add fake typos.

Return the finished version unless the user asks for critique.
